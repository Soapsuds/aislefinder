AisleFinder — Color Scheme Options

Four directions that keep the green identity. Every option softens the dark mode: the background gets a deliberate tint (forest or warm charcoal) instead of neutral near-black, the accent green is desaturated so it doesn't glow, and big headings use calm mint or cream instead of bright green.

Current (for comparison)

Reference

Pure white light mode with lemon amber; dark mode is neutral near-black with a saturated neon green (#3fd07f) — the combination that tends to read harsh and "terminal-like."

Option 1 — Warm Cream & Forest

Warm / organic

Light mode swaps stark white for a warm cream page with white cards floating on it, and the amber warms up from lemon to honey. Dark mode is a deep forest green instead of gray — the whole screen feels intentionally green rather than "black app with green buttons." Text is warm off-white (cream) to match.

Option 2 — Evergreen Chrome

Most distinctive

The top bar and bottom nav become solid forest green in both modes, giving the app a strong identity — the biggest visual change of the four. Body stays light and calm; amber becomes the active-tab highlight against the green chrome. Dark mode keeps the green header on a soft dark body, so it never reads as flat black.

Option 3 — Sage & Stone

Calmest

Everything desaturates a notch: sage-washed light background, a quieter garden green (#3e9d63) instead of the bright Kroger-esque green, muted amber. Dark mode is a warm charcoal with a soft matte mint accent — the gentlest dark mode of the four, good if the current one feels garish.

Option 4 — Market Fresh

Boldest green

Leans into green harder: mint-tinted cards on white, and aisle headers become filled green pills so the structure of the sorted list pops. Dark mode is deep evergreen with pill headers in a muted forest — the green does the organizing, not just the accenting.

Navy Variants

Same four directions, same background warmth and amber accent — the green hue is rotated to navy blue everywhere it was doing work (buttons, checkmarks, pills, headings, chrome). Lightness and saturation relationships carry over 1:1 from each green option, so these are direct swaps, not new moods.

Option 1N — Warm Cream & Navy

Warm / organic

Navy counterpart to Warm Cream & Forest. Same cream page and honey amber; the forest green becomes a mid navy blue, and dark mode swaps the deep-forest tint for a deep-navy one while keeping the warm cream text.

Option 2N — Navy Chrome

Most distinctive

Navy counterpart to Evergreen Chrome. Top bar and bottom nav become solid navy in both modes, with amber still doing the active-tab highlight against the blue chrome.

Option 3N — Slate & Stone

Calmest

Navy counterpart to Sage & Stone. The garden green becomes a quiet slate blue at the same desaturation level; dark mode keeps the warm charcoal body with a soft periwinkle accent instead of matte mint.

Option 4N — Ocean Fresh

Boldest blue

Navy counterpart to Market Fresh. Sky-tinted cards on white, and aisle headers become filled navy pills. Dark mode is deep marine blue with pill headers in muted navy.

Ocean Fresh — Amber Placement

Same Ocean Fresh palette, unchanged — these five only move where a touch of amber shows up, to find the spot that feels right without letting it fight the navy. In dark mode the amber is a deeper gold (#d99a2b) rather than a pale tint, since a light amber sitting on a dark background reads washed out.

4N-a — Icons Only

Control

Amber appears only on the star icon and the progress-pill icon. The progress pill itself stays navy, matching the aisle tags.

4N-b — Amber Badge

Warmest

The "done" progress pill itself picks up an amber tint (fill + text), making it read as a little reward badge against all the surrounding navy.

4N-c — Amber Trim

Subtlest

Aisle-tag pills get a thin amber border instead of navy — a quiet warm edge on an otherwise all-navy tag. Progress pill stays navy like the control.

4N-d — Amber Ring

Playful

Unchecked circles get an amber outline instead of a neutral one — items "waiting to be checked" glow warm, then switch to a filled navy check when done.

4N-e — Amber Nav

Most structural

The active bottom-nav tab is amber instead of navy, echoing how Navy Chrome uses amber for its active-tab highlight against the blue chrome.
