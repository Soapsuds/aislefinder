Aisles & pins — 20 concepts

Every concept on this page works the same two ingredients: a corridor receding to a vanishing point and an amber location pin. These are variations on the DNA of the shipped mark in src/components/Logo.jsx, pushed in different directions — perspective depth, top-down maps, pure reduction. Palette per docs/design-rules.html: greens + one amber, no blue.

App icon concepts — 12, icon only

Fixed dark-green tile (matching the top-bar chrome), white/mint structure, amber always and only for the pin or destination.

01 Pin at the vanishing point

Two aisle walls converge and the pin sits exactly where they meet — the destination literally is the point of the perspective.

02 Floor grid

The supermarket floor drawn as a perspective grid, tiles compressing toward the back, with the pin standing mid-aisle where your item is.

03 Nested doorways

Walking down the aisle as nested frames shrinking to the center — depth without any diagonal lines. The amber dot is the far end of the trip.

04 Aisle inside the pin

Inversion of 01: the pin is the container and the receding aisle lives inside its head. Strong silhouette for small sizes.

05 Hanging aisle signs

The overhead aisle placards receding down the corridor — the most literal "grocery store" image on the page — with the pin dropped under the nearest one.

06 Store map, top down

Aisles seen from above as three bars, with the dashed route snaking through the gaps to the pin. The planner's view instead of the shopper's.

07 A with a pin heart

The letter A as two receding aisle walls, its counter filled by the pin instead of a crossbar. Monogram, aisle, and destination in one glyph.

08 Ceiling lights

The fluorescent strips overhead, shrinking and fading with distance, funneling the eye straight down to the pin at the end of the run.

09 Long-shadow pin

The pin casts a shadow that widens away from it like an aisle in reverse perspective — the recede happens on the floor, not the walls.

10 Route through the corridor

The walls fade to thin guides and the dashed route becomes the hero, curving up the aisle to the pin. Closest evolution of the shipped mark.

11 Barcode aisle

Shelf uprights read as barcode bars, stepping down toward a central gap where the pin waits — the aisle opening drawn in the store's own material.

12 The reduction

Two strokes and a dot: the whole idea at its smallest. This is the version that survives a 16px favicon and a monochrome watch face.
